## Keys & Commits

_Some of our biggest projects in Q3 focused on keys and their relationship to identity and the rest of the digital ecosystem._

<a href="https://github.com/BlockchainCommons/passkey-cloudflare"><img src="https://developer.blockchaincommons.com/assets/badges/cloudflare-passkey.png" style="float: right" width=200px></a>

**Cloudflare Passkeys.** Blockchain Commons debuted its new Passkey-only identity, [passkey-cloudflare](https://github.com/BlockchainCommons/passkey-cloudflare), which is also available as a [live demo](https://passkeydemo.gordianstack.com/). The Passkeys verify an identity, with multiple Passkeys available to any user. We hope to use this as the foundation of authentication in [CSR](https://developer.blockchaincommons.com/csr/) and other systems.

<a href="https://developer.blockchaincommons.com/open-integrity/"><img src="https://developer.blockchaincommons.com/assets/badges/open-integrity.png" style="float: right" width=200px></a>

**Keylay Demo.** We're always happy to spotlight other work being done in the ecosystem that supports the self-sovereignty of users, so we were happy in August to host a [demo](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oAsxoo3Rqn4) of [Keylay](https://keylay.org/), an online multisig coordinator that can make resilient control of Bitcoin assets into a reality.

**Open Integrity / AI.** At the [Gordian meeting](https://www.blockchaincommons.com/subscribe/) where we talked about the new Passkey system, we also [opened a discussion](https://developer.blockchaincommons.com/meetings/2026-10-passkeys/) on how we should be recording the use of AI in Git repos. For us, much of this bound up in the use of [Open Integrity](https://developer.blockchaincommons.com/open-integrity/), a system that improves the accountability of Git repos, but it grows more complex when an AI with a key can sign a thousand things (or more!) in a day. One of our suggestions? AI can only make changes to branches, which may be incorporated into `main` only by humans who verify everything.

## New Libraries

<a href="https://developer.blockchaincommons.com/libraries/"><img src="https://developer.blockchaincommons.com/assets/badges/libraries.png" style="float: right" width=200px></a>

_Blockchain Commons' [libraries](https://developer.blockchaincommons.com/libraries/) make the Gordian Stack available to developers._

**Typescript.** Leonardo Custodio and Parity Technologies have been offering some great support for Blockchain Commons for a while. This quarter, they started pushing over the typescript libraries they've created, so that we can offer them to the community. Things kicked off with [bc-dcbor-ts](https://github.com/BlockchainCommons/bc-dcbor-ts) early in the quarter, but as of this writing they've shared almost [20 typescript libraries](https://github.com/orgs/BlockchainCommons/repositories?language=&q=-ts&sort=&type=all), covering most of the Gordian Stack, including foundational technologies such as [SSKR](https://github.com/BlockchainCommons/bc-sskr-ts), [Lifehash](https://github.com/BlockchainCommons/bc-lifehash-ts), and [Gordian Envelope](https://github.com/BlockchainCommons/bc-envelope-ts).

## Learning Bitcoin

<a href="https://learningbitcoin.blockchaincommons.com/"><img src="https://developer.blockchaincommons.com/assets/badges/learning-bitcoin.png" style="float: right" width=200px></a>

_We have been continuing work on a new edition of [Learning Bitcoin from the Command Line](https://learningbitcoin.blockchaincommons.com/), our very popular Bitcoin developer course, thanks to a grant from [HRF](https://hrf.org/)._

_Besides the revisionary work, getting everything up to date with Bitcoin Core 31.1, we've also been releasing brand-new chapters on new concepts._

**Sharding Secrets.** Last quarter we mentioned our first new chapter, ["Working with Secrets"](https://learningbitcoin.blockchaincommons.com/10_0_Working_with_Secrets/), which demonstrates how to use technologies like [Gordian Envelope](https://developer.blockchaincommons.com/envelope/) and the [Gordian Reference Apps](https://developer.blockchaincommons.com/apps/) to make your assets more resilient. We thought it powerful enough that we've since [turned it into a video presentation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ckUwWCFqua8).

**Miniscript.** Our second full new chapter is ["Using Miniscript"](https://learningbitcoin.blockchaincommons.com/16_0_Using_Miniscript/), which talks about the new(er) ways to represent Bitcoin Script: Miniscript and Policy. This also allowed us to introduce the BDK toolkit, another great command-line tool for Bitcoin.

**Taproot.** Our final big expansion for Learning Bitcoin is a series of chapters on Taproot and the new signatures it supports such as FROST and MuSig2. Our [introductory chapter](https://learningbitcoin.blockchaincommons.com/17_0_Introducing_Taproot/) is fully available, while we're working on closing out work on MuSig2 and Tapscript sections.

**Bitcoin Standup.** Finally, Learning Bitcoin work has again caused us to upgrade our [Bitcoin Standup Scripts](https://github.com/BlockchainCommons/Bitcoin-Standup-Scripts), this time to Bitcoin Core 31.1.

## Updating Dev

<a href="https://developer.blockchaincommons.com/"><img src="https://developer.blockchaincommons.com/assets/badges/dev-pages.png" style="float: right" width=200px></a>

_Our [Developer Pages](https://developer.blockchaincommons.com/) have long been a top resource for people wanting to adopt Gordian Stack technologies. We largely finished our massive revamp of these web pages in Q3._

**Technology.** Our new [technology section](https://developer.blockchaincommons.com/technology/) is done. Along with our overview video, it includes categories for data formats, data resilience, digital identity, digital signatures, and secure communications, plus Blockchain specific work (currently limited to Bitcoin, Ethereum, and Zcash). We had this underway in Q2 and are thrilled to have closed it out (and hope the new organization is clearer than our previous attempt to pile everything up in the same stack).

**Architecture.** Tech is just the front-facing part of Blockchain Commons' work. It's all based on specific architectural designs. Our new [architecture page](https://developer.blockchaincommons.com/architecture/) talks about how we build up from principles to philosophies to patterns, mirroring our concepts in both software and network design.

**Touchpoints.** We're aware that the Gordian Stack is pretty huge. In less than 10 years we've developed technologies to solve a variety of problems related to independence, resilience, privacy, and openness, and it can be hard to figure out how to get started with it all! As a result, we're developing a series of [Touchpoints](https://developer.blockchaincommons.com/#touchpoints) meant to highlight relevant technologies for a variety of developers. Our first one spotlights [technologies for self-sovereign identity designers](https://developer.blockchaincommons.com/touchpoints/ssi/). Though the rest of the developer pages are 99% done in their updated form, we expect to add more touchpoints in the coming months. (If there's one you want to see, [let us know](mailto:team@blockchaincommons.com))
